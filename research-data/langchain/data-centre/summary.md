**Executive Summary – Data‑Centre Landscape in the MEA Region**

The Middle East and Africa (MEA) data‑centre market is expanding at a healthy 8.5 % CAGR, with installed capacity expected to rise from 3,200 MW in 2023 to 4,100 MW by 2024. New construction and edge‑site deployments are growing at 9.2 % and 7.5 % respectively, driven by digital transformation across finance, energy, and public‑sector sectors. Hybrid‑cloud spend is projected to hit $12 bn by 2025, while edge‑computing investments are poised to grow at 15 % CAGR, powered by 5G roll‑outs and IoT proliferation. These dynamics create a compelling opportunity for organisations to invest in Tier III/IV facilities that deliver N+1 redundancy, sub‑1.4 PUE, and 10 ms latency to end‑users.

Technical best practices are converging around high‑density racks (10–20 kW), dedicated 480 V circuits, and advanced airflow management that maintains a 70 % free‑cooling ratio. Tier III designs now demand concurrent maintainability and separate power/cooling paths, while Tier IV targets PUE of 1.25–1.30 and 30 % renewable energy sourcing—above the regional average of 20 %. Regulatory pressures are also tightening; data‑localisation mandates in the UAE, Egypt, and Kenya, coupled with green‑building standards, necessitate design changes that balance compliance with cost‑efficiency.

Sustainability is becoming a differentiator: achieving PUE below 1.3 is no longer a luxury but a competitive requirement, and renewable‑energy procurement can reduce operating costs by 15–20 % over the life of a facility. Moreover, regulatory compliance spend—estimated at $500 m annually—will continue to rise, offering a clear incentive for early adoption of robust security and privacy frameworks.

**Strategic Recommendations**

1. **Accelerate Tier III/IV deployments** in GCC and North‑African hubs to capture the projected $3 bn share of global data‑centre spend and meet local data‑localisation mandates.  
2. **Invest in edge‑capable infrastructure** that can support 5G‑backed services and IoT workloads, targeting a 15 % CAGR in edge deployments.  
3. **Prioritise PUE optimisation and renewable sourcing** to reduce CAPEX/OMAX and comply with emerging green‑building regulations—aim for 1.25 PUE and 30 % renewable share within 3–5 years.  
4. **Embed regulatory compliance into the design phase**, leveraging Uptime Institute’s Tier Standards and local data‑protection laws to minimise retrofitting costs.  

By aligning investment in scalable, energy‑efficient, and regulatory‑compliant data‑centres with the region’s accelerating digital agenda, organisations can secure a resilient, cost‑effective foundation for their cloud, edge, and connectivity strategies.
